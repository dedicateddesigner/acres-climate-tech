## 04 — What is Biochar?

Headline:

What is Biochar?

Body:

Biochar is a stable, carbon-rich material produced by heating organic matter — such as crop residue or agricultural waste — in a low-oxygen environment through a process called pyrolysis. Instead of that biomass decomposing or being burned (both of which release carbon back into the atmosphere), pyrolysis locks the carbon into a solid form that can remain stable in soil for hundreds to thousands of years.

When applied to farmland, biochar does more than store carbon. It improves soil structure, boosts water retention, supports beneficial soil microbes, and can improve long-term yields — making it a rare solution that benefits the climate and the farmer at the same time.

Process:

Biomass → Pyrolysis → Biochar → Soil Application → Carbon Locked

## 05 — What is Enhanced Rock Weathering?

Headline:

What is Enhanced Rock Weathering?

Body:

Enhanced Rock Weathering accelerates a natural geological process. Certain silicate rocks — like basalt — naturally absorb CO₂ from the atmosphere as they weather, a process that normally takes thousands of years. By grinding these rocks into fine particles and spreading them on agricultural soil, ERW speeds up this reaction dramatically.

As the rock particles weather, they draw down atmospheric CO₂ and convert it into stable minerals, while also releasing beneficial nutrients into the soil — improving fertility as a byproduct of carbon removal.

Process:

Rock → Crushing → Application on Field → Weathering Reaction → CO₂ Drawdown
